Pani Puri

DESCRIPTION

Panipuri (also known by other names, including phuchka and golgappa) is a snack associated with the cuisines of the Indian subcontinent consisting of a deep-fried spherical puri shell, hollowed out for a filling and dipped in flavoured waters.

Panipuri is primarily a street food and is part of the chaat category of light snacks. It is commonly filled with some combination of potatoes, chickpeas, spices, and chutney. The flavoured waters, or pani, are typically a spicy coriander leaf or mint chutney called teekha pani and a sweet tamarind chutney called meetha pani.

INGREDIENTS

  • Semolina (sooji), all-purpose flour (maida), oil, and warm water.
  • Baking soda and salt
  • Fresh mint leaves and coriander leaves
  • Green chilies and fresh ginger
  • Tamarind pulp and jaggery
  • Black salt (kala namak), roasted cumin powder, and chaat masala.
  • Cold water and boond
  • Boiled and mashed potato

STEPS

  1. Blend herbs: Grind 1/2 cup mint leaves, 1 cup coriander leaves, 1-inch ginger, and 2 green chilies into a smooth paste with a little water.
  2. Mix water: Transfer the paste to a large bowl with 4 cups of cold water.
  3. Season: Stir in 1 tsp roasted cumin powder, 1 tsp chaat masala, 1/2 tsp black salt, and regular salt to taste. Add a handful of boondi
  4. Mash potatoes: Boil 2 medium potatoes, peel them, and mash them well in a bowl.
  5. Add ingredients: Mix in 1 cup of boiled chickpeas and 1/4 cup of finely chopped red onions.
  6. Spice it up: Season with a pinch of salt, cumin powder, and chaat masala, then mix everything togethe
  7. Crack the puri: Gently tap the top of a crispy ready-made or homemade puri to make a small hole.
  8. Stuff it: Spoon a small amount of the potato and chickpea filling inside
  9. Add chutney: Add a tiny drop of sweet tamarind chutney if desired
  10. Dip and eat: Dip the stuffed puri into the spiced water (pani) and eat it immediately in one bite
